Master task: Step distillation for on-device image generation

Goal

- Cut the number of denoising steps a text-to-image generator needs, so it can produce high-quality images on mobile and edge hardware within tight latency, memory and energy budgets.
- Recent work shows that 1–4 step generation is now achievable for modern flow-matching transformer (DiT) models.

Literature baseline

- SANA-Sprint (Chen et al., 2025)
-- Converts a pretrained flow-matching model into TrigFlow form without retraining, which is what continuous-time consistency distillation (sCM) needs.
-- Combines sCM with latent adversarial distillation (LADD) in one student that works at 1, 2 or 4 steps.
-- Generates a 1024×1024 image in about 0.1 s on an H100 and 0.31 s on an RTX 4090.
-- Needs a short teacher fine-tune (dense timestep embeddings, QK-normalisation) to keep training stable.

- SiD-DiT (Zhou et al., 2025)
-- Extends score identity distillation to flow-matching DiTs from 0.6B to 12B parameters (SANA, SD3/3.5, FLUX.1-dev).
-- Uses one shared configuration, with no teacher fine-tuning and no real training images.
-- Its 4-step students match or beat their teachers on FID and GenEval.
-- Optional adversarial training on real data improves FID further.

- Practical guide (Pu et al., 2025)
-- Compares sCM and MeanFlow on an 8B FLUX.1-lite teacher.
-- sCM stays close to teacher quality at 1–2 steps; MeanFlow needs about 4 steps but then gives sharper detail and stronger prompt alignment.
-- Gives practical recipes, such as rescaling timesteps to [0, 1] and distilling from the teacher’s guided velocity, and releases open-source code.

Gap: all three report results on datacenter or desktop GPUs. None measures on mobile or edge devices.

Scope of work

- Apply and compare these methods (sCM+LADD, SiD, MeanFlow) on compact, deployment-friendly teachers.
- Choose the step count for each target device’s latency budget: 1–2 steps (favours sCM) or 4 steps (favours MeanFlow or SiD).
- Make the students deployable:
- quantisation-aware training (e.g. INT8/INT4)
- operator and memory-layout optimisation for target NPUs/GPUs
- conversion to on-device runtimes

Evaluation

- Quality: FID, CLIP score, GenEval/DPG-Bench, human preference studies.
- Efficiency: per-image latency, peak memory and power draw on representative phones and edge devices.
